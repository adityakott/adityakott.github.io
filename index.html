01 / ABOUT

A little about me.

I am an atmospheric scientist interested in the physical mechanisms governing tropical intraseasonal variability and monsoon dynamics.

My academic training began with a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering from Vignan Lara Institute of Technology, Guntur (2015), followed by an M.Tech in Aerospace Engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology Madras (2018). I went on to pursue a PhD in Atmospheric and Oceanic Sciences at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru, completing it in 2024. This path from engineering to atmospheric science shaped my interest in fluid dynamics across a wide range of scales, from aircraft-scale flows to planetary-scale atmospheric circulation.

Following my PhD, I worked as a Research Associate at the Centre for Atmospheric and Oceanic Sciences (CAOS), IISc, in 2025. I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (HUJI), working with Professor Ori Adam.

My research focuses on tropical atmospheric dynamics, intraseasonal variability, convection and monsoon systems. I am particularly interested in the physical mechanisms governing the propagation and evolution of the Boreal Summer Intraseasonal Oscillation (BSISO) and the Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO), and in how interactions between tropical convection, large-scale circulation and atmospheric moisture shape these phenomena.

To investigate these processes, I combine observational and reanalysis datasets with climate-model simulations and theoretical dynamical frameworks. My work draws on moisture and energy budget analyses, Weak Temperature Gradient (WTG) theory and Gross Moist Stability (GMS) to examine the dynamical and thermodynamic constraints on tropical convection and its propagation.

My recent research examines how changes in tropical convection and background thermodynamic conditions influence BSISO behaviour and associated rainfall variability in a warming climate. I am also interested in changes in monsoon rainfall extremes and the physical processes underlying their variability.

Beyond atmospheric science, I maintain a strong interest in computational fluid dynamics, particularly finite-volume methods, and in developing physically grounded explanations for fluid and climate dynamics.
