01 / ABOUT

A little about me.

My research lies at the intersection of tropical atmospheric dynamics, thermodynamics and climate variability.

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (HUJI), working with Professor Ori Adam. I received my PhD in Atmospheric and Oceanic Sciences from the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru, where my doctoral research focused on the Boreal Summer Intraseasonal Oscillation (BSISO).

My research broadly focuses on tropical atmospheric dynamics, intraseasonal variability, convection and monsoon systems. I am particularly interested in the physical mechanisms governing the propagation and evolution of the BSISO and the Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO), and in how interactions between tropical convection, large-scale circulation and atmospheric moisture shape these phenomena.

To investigate these processes, I combine observational and reanalysis datasets with climate-model simulations and theoretical dynamical frameworks. My work draws on moisture and energy budget analyses, Weak Temperature Gradient (WTG) theory and Gross Moist Stability (GMS) to examine the dynamical and thermodynamic constraints on tropical convection and its propagation.

My recent research examines how changes in tropical convection and background thermodynamic conditions influence BSISO behaviour and associated rainfall variability in a warming climate. I am also interested in changes in monsoon rainfall extremes and the physical processes underlying their variability.

Beyond atmospheric science, I maintain a strong interest in fluid dynamics and computational approaches to understanding physical processes in the atmosphere.
